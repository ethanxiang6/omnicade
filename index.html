    Why most sites can't be embedded

    Response headers · measured, not guessed

    Most game sites
    refuse to be embedded.

    An <iframe> only loads if the destination permits it. Send X-Frame-Options or a restrictive frame-ancestors directive and every browser refuses — the page never renders, and no amount of local JavaScript can override it. It is the server's decision, enforced on your machine. So every destination in this cabinet was probed first. These are the real headers:

    curl -sSI · 2026-09-16
    OriginHeader returnedIn an iframe
    Blocked origins still open in a normal tab — the player offers that instead of showing you a dead frame.

    Messages

    Chats.

    The button in the bottom-right corner opens your messages — on this page and over a game, windowed or fullscreen. Conversations, friends and nicknames are saved to your account, so they follow you to any device you sign in on.

    Friend requests

    Search someone by email and send a request. They have to accept before either of you can send a message, and requests wait in a folder at the top of your list.

    Private nicknames

    Rename anyone in your chats. The name you pick is yours alone — the other person never sees it, and neither do admins.

    Unread dots

    A red dot sits on the chat button when someone messages you, and on that person's row inside the list, so you can see who it came from at a glance.

    Admins

    Admins can start a chat with anyone without a request, and always appear as "Admin" — that one name can't be changed.

    Safety: an admin can read any conversation on this site, including private messages between two people.
